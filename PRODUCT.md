# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two roles, and only two: the **administrator** (the proprietor of Nicholas Nickland
Enterprise and whoever they trust with the books) and the **Sales Person**. They sell medical
and laboratory supplies in Ghana — analysers, reagents, consumables — to hospitals and
laboratories, sourcing much of it from suppliers only after a customer has ordered.

They work at a desk on a Windows computer, often offline, in a bright office or a stockroom;
the counter and the receiving bench are used by touch. The jobs: build a costed quotation
from several suppliers' prices, turn the customer's acceptance into an order, buy and receive
what it needs, assemble and dispatch it, collect the money, and keep a ledger an accountant
will sign. A Sales Person must never see a supplier's cost or the margin.

## Product Purpose

Nickland ERP is an offline-first business platform for procurement, sales, point of sale,
inventory, dispatch and finance, built first for one medical-supplies distributor and
configurable for others. It exists so that a quotation, the order it becomes, the stock it
moves and the money it earns are one connected record, every figure traceable to its source
and every document reproducible as it was sent.

Success is a proprietor who trusts the numbers without re-checking them, documents that look
as good as the pro forma invoices they already send, and staff who can do a day's work
without asking how.

## Brand Personality

Professional, precise, composed. The interface is an instrument of work, not a presentation
about work. It should feel human and well made — the confidence of a good accountant's
ledger and a well-run stockroom — never playful and never showy. Titles, headings and labels
use formal vocabulary ("Issue quotation", "Record receipt", "Confirm delivery"), say what
they do, and carry no unnecessary explanation.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards: hero metrics in oversized cards, gradient accents, identical card
  grids, a big number with a tiny label and no period or unit.
- Decorative gradients, orange as a page background, glassmorphism, illustrations, mascots,
  stock photography.
- Consumer-app playfulness: exclamation marks, emoji, chatty microcopy ("Let's send it!").
- Placeholder, sample or filler content in shipped screens.
- Long explanatory paragraphs on a working screen.
- Colour as the only signal of meaning, or brand colour standing in for status.

## Design Principles

1. **State the next action.** Every working screen has one obvious next step, labelled for
   what it does; transactional screens carry one large primary action.
2. **Every figure has a source and a unit.** Amounts carry their currency, quantities their
   unit, measures their period; nothing is computed in the interface.
3. **Dense where it aids comprehension, quiet everywhere else.** Tables over cards for data;
   whitespace and type, not boxes, for hierarchy.
4. **Touch where hands are busy.** The counter, the receiving bench and the dispatch bay get
   large, forgiving controls; the desk gets precise ones.
5. **What was sent is what is kept.** Documents mirror the business's own pro forma style and
   reprint byte for byte; nothing on screen contradicts the record.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA throughout: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and component boundaries;
status always carries a label and a shape as well as colour; every workflow completes by
keyboard with a visible focus ring; usable at 200% zoom and at 125% and 150% Windows display
scaling; reduced-motion respected; custom themes are refused if they fail contrast. Touch
targets are at least 44px, 56–64px on transactional controls.
